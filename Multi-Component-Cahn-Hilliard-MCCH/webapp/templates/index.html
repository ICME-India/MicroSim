📖 Generalized Multi-Component Cahn-Hilliard (MCCH) Theory
1. Governing Equations

The Generalized Multi-Component Cahn-Hilliard model describes isothermal phase separation and diffuse interface evolution in $N$-component systems subject to the partition of unity constraint:

∑_{i=0}^{N-1} c_i(x, t) = 1

The total Helmholtz / Gibbs free energy functional is defined as the sum of homogeneous bulk free energy density $f_0(\mathbf{c})$ and interface gradient energy:

F[\mathbf{c}] = ∫_Ω [ f_0(\mathbf{c}) + ½ ∑_{i,j=0}^{N-1} κ_{ij} ∇c_i · ∇c_j ] dΩ
2. Variational Chemical Potentials & Projection

The variational derivative yields raw chemical potentials $\mu_i$:

μ_i = δF/δc_i = ∂f_0/∂c_i - ∑_{j=0}^{N-1} κ_{ij} ∇²c_j

To enforce the Gibbs-Duhem relation and maintain $\sum c_i = 1$, the projected diffusion chemical potentials are computed by removing the mean chemical potential:

μ̃_i = μ_i - μ̄, where μ̄ = (1 / N) ∑_{k=0}^{N-1} μ_k
3. Onsager Mobilities & Evolution

Mass conservation follows the continuity equation:

∂c_i/∂t = -∇ · J_i = ∇ · [ ∑_j M_{ij}(\mathbf{c}) ∇μ̃_j ]
  • Constant Mobility: $M_{ij} = M_0 \delta_{ij} \implies \partial c_i / \partial t = M_0 \nabla^2 \tilde{\mu}_i$.
  • Degenerate Onsager Mobility: $M_{ij}(\mathbf{c}) = M_0 (c_i \delta_{ij} - c_i c_j)$, guaranteeing zero flux when $c_i = 0$ and satisfying $\sum_i \mathbf{J}_i = \mathbf{0}$.
4. Slab Domain Decomposition Architecture (MPI)

In 1D slab decomposition, the computational domain ($N_x \times N_y \times N_z$) is partitioned along the primary $x$-axis among $P$ MPI ranks:

Global Domain [Nx x Ny x Nz]:
| Rank 0 Slab [0 .. Nx/4-1] | Rank 1 Slab [Nx/4 .. Nx/2-1] | Rank 2 Slab [Nx/2 .. 3Nx/4-1] | Rank 3 Slab [3Nx/4 .. Nx-1] |
Contiguous memory slices in row-major C/C++ allow direct zero-copy MPI_Isend / MPI_Irecv ghost exchange!
